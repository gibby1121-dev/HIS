# D. The Customer — 58-year-old owner-operator, one truck, 30 years

*Prompt: you have never used an online auction. Walk through this experience and
say exactly where you'd quit, get confused, or refuse.*

---

I'm selling the '18 T880. Bought it new, 640,000 on it, I know every bolt.
Knee's done, my back's done, and my boy went into HVAC. I owe about $18,400 on
it at the bank. I want $92,000. A guy at the truck stop said his brother got
$95 for one with more miles, so I know what it's worth.

Here's where you lose me.

---

**"Are you posting, or are you selling?"**

I pick posting. Every time. It's free, and the other one sounds like signing
something.

I'm not being cute. I don't know yet whether I'm selling. I want to see what
happens first. And "selling" means an obligation, and I've been burned enough
times by things I signed without reading that my default answer to any button
that implies a contract is *no*.

You should assume every guy like me picks the free one. If your whole business
is the paid one, that question, asked that way, is the end of the funnel. It
doesn't feel like a choice between two products. It feels like a choice between
*nothing* and *committing to something I don't understand*.

---

**"Set your private reserve."**

Two problems.

First, "reserve" means auction. And I already decided I'm not doing an auction,
because auctions are where you get robbed — you sit there and watch three
dealers not bid and then it goes for $60. My cousin sold a skid loader that way
in '19 and still talks about it. If I figure out this is an auction, I close the
window.

Second, and this is the real one: **I don't know my floor and I'm not typing it
into a website.** It's not a number I have. It's a feeling that depends on
whether the bank's on me, what my wife says in March, and whether the guy asking
is decent. If you force me to type something, I'll type $92,000 — my want price
— because that's the only number in my head, and because I'm not showing my hand
to a computer.

So now your mechanism has a fake reserve in it, and everything downstream is
built on a number I made up to get past your form.

---

**"Your price will publicly decrease on this schedule."**

No.

This is the one where I stop and don't come back, and it's got nothing to do
with money. Everybody sees that. The guys at the co-op, the fella who's been
after me to sell it to him for two years, my neighbor. It reads like a sign in
my yard that says I'M GETTING DESPERATE, and it counts down where they can watch.

I'd rather sit on it for a year than have that be public. I know that's not
rational. I don't care. That truck's got my name on the door.

If the price were dropping *quietly* — if it were you calling me and saying
"Kent, I think we ought to try eighty-eight" — that's completely different. Same
number, same schedule. But it's between me and you, and it's your idea, not my
public surrender.

---

**"Your Buyer Engagement Score is 34. Top-of-funnel failure."**

I don't know what this means and I'm not going to ask, because asking makes me
look stupid.

The photos thing — I took four with my phone. One's the tire, because the tires
are new and that cost me $3,200. If you tell me my photos are the problem, my
honest reaction is that the photos are *fine*, you can see the whole truck. I am
not going back out to the lot to retake pictures. It's 14 degrees.

What works: "Bill's gonna swing by Thursday morning and shoot it, takes twenty
minutes, no charge." Now it's done and I didn't do it.

The number that would actually get through to me isn't a score. It's this:

> "That truck's costing you $412 a month sitting there — note, insurance, and
> what it's losing in value. You've been sitting eleven weeks. That's $1,100
> you've already spent to hold out for $4,000."

Nobody's ever put it to me that way. That'd make me quiet for a minute. Say it
in dollars, per month, out of my pocket. Don't call it carrying cost. Don't put
it on a dashboard. Say it to me on the phone.

---

**The account, the password, the dashboard**

I'll do a text message with one link and one button. My daughter set up my
email; I know the password because it's written inside the cabinet door.

I will not create an account. I will not download an app. If there's a
verification code step I'm going to call somebody, and if nobody picks up, I'm
done for the day and probably for good.

**And there had better be a phone number on every screen.** If I can't find a
number in ten seconds I assume it's a scam, because the last three things that
had no phone number were.

---

**The part you're underrating: the bank**

Everybody keeps talking about price. Here's what actually scares me.

I owe $18,400. The bank's got the title. I don't know how that works when I
sell it. Does the buyer pay me and then I pay the bank? What if he pays me and
the check's no good and now I've signed the title over? What if he shows up with
a trailer and cash and I'm standing in the yard trying to figure out if it's
real money?

I want the money in my hand — or in my account, confirmed, with somebody I can
call — **before that truck leaves my lot.** And I need somebody to handle the
bank part, because I've never done it and I don't want to learn.

Solve that and I'll put up with a lot. Don't solve it and none of the rest
matters, because I'll just keep the truck.

---

## Where I'd quit, in order

1. **The posting/selling question** — I pick free, and you never see me again.
2. **Typing my floor into a form** — I refuse, or I lie.
3. **The public countdown** — pride. Hard stop, no negotiation.
4. **Any account creation or password step** — I wander off.
5. **No phone number** — I assume scam.
6. **The score/dashboard** — I don't quit, I just ignore it entirely.

## What would actually sell me on this

Kent calls me. I know Kent, or I know somebody who does. He says:

> "I'll handle the bank and the title. You'll have your money before it leaves
> the yard. Here's what I think it brings and here's what it's costing you every
> month to wait. We'll start at ninety-two, and if it doesn't move I'm going to
> call you in three weeks about eighty-eight — you tell me yes or no then."

That's the exact same mechanism. Descending price, committed seller, private
floor. I'd sign that today.

**The difference isn't the mechanism. It's that a person I trust is carrying it,
it's not public, and I never had to understand any of it.**

If that's true, then what you're building isn't a website I use. It's the thing
that tells Kent what to say to me, and when to call.
